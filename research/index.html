Research

SMEL is a sister laboratory of the Emissions and Fuels Research (EFR) Group at the College of Engineering, Center for Environmental Research and Technology (CE-CERT). The applications of my research extends from brake and tire wear particulate emissions from light-duty vehicles, heavy-duty trucks, and trains, vehicle and maritime emissions, air pollution impacts from battery fires, and emission mitigation strategies.

Research focus areas

Non-Exhaust Emissions from Mobile Sources

We develop novel samping systems for onboard vehicle testing to measure brake-wear and tire and road wear particle and gaseous emissions from light-duty and commercial vehicles. We study how brake pad material, tire type, road surface, and driving dynamics affect the formation of non-exhaust emissions. Our research examines airborne microplastic pollution released into the environment from tire-wear, along with other potentially toxic pollutants. My research is supported by the California Air Resources Board (CARB), South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD), the Center for Efficient Mobility, the National Center for Sustainavble Transportation (NCST), which is a U.S. Department of Transportation national University Transportation Center, and the automotive industry.

Hydrogen and Hydrogen Blends with Renewable Natural Gas for Transportation Engines and Non-Road Applications

Hydrogen is a promising zero-carbon fuel for transportation engines and non-road applications (e.g., power generation, construction and agricultural equipment, etc.). We tested and demonstrated a hydrogen internal combustion engine vehicle both on-road and in the laboratory, marking the first demonstration of its kind in the U.S. Our lab has also conducted projects evaluating the combustion performance and emissions of hydrogen-RNG blends in a heavy-duty ultra-low NOx highway engine using advanced diagnostic techniques. Our research is supported by the South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD), Sacramento Metro Air Quality Management District, Southern California Gas Company, and Pacific Gas and Electric Company (PG&E). - text: Example Emissions from natural gas heavy-duty engines (demo)

Low-Carbon Liquid Biofuels

We have extensively studied the environmental impacts of low-carbon liquid biofuels in both spark-ignition and compression-ignition engines. Resarch in this area includes the use of higher alcohols blended with gasoline, such as iso-butanol blends, low-, mid-, and high-concentration ethanol blends for conventional and flexible fuel vehicles, and renewable naphtha blends with ethanol and gasoline. We have investigated the effects of gasoline composition and particulate matter index (PMI) on the formation of toxic pollutants and primary particulate emissions, as well as the production of secondary organic aerosol, using environmental chambers and oxidation flow reactors. I led a landmark study demonstrating significant emissions reductions from the use of E15 fuel in California. This work contributed to the adoption of legislation allowing gasoline blends containing up to 15% ethanol by volume in the California market. My lab has also conducted one the first studies using renewable naphtha blended with gasoline and ethanol to investigate criteria emissions and secondary aerosol formation, along with a detailed life cycle analysis. Over the years, our research has been supported by the California Air Resources Board (CARB), South Coast Air Quality Management District (SCAQMD), USCAR, Coordinating Research Council, California Energy Commission, Growth Energy, Renewable Fuels Association, ICM Inc, National Corn Growers Association, Pearson Fuels, and others.
